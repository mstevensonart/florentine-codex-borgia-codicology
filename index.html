About this project

Both manuscripts were examined in person — the Codex Borgia at the Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, the Florentine Codex at the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana in Florence — using magnification and, for the Florentine Codex, both daylight and ultraviolet light. The originating research was conducted for an M.A. thesis in Art History at Arizona State University (2003, chair: Emily Umberger), with fieldwork and writing continuing intermittently since.

This site exists because the underlying evidence base is more extensive and more rigorous than any single journal article can show: every claim made in the essay traces back to a specific folio, panel, or leaf record, browsable here in full.
